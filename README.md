
<h1 align="center">From Farm to Cup</h1>

<p align="center"><em>An interactive Power BI report that tells the story from farm to cup. For anyone curious about coffee, whether you drink it or just love the smell.</em></p>

---

| Page                    | What you will find                                                                                                                 |
| ----------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Cover**               | Data Art made from the coffee CSV files with Python, SHA3                                                                          |
| **Overview**            | Arabica, Robusta and other coffees worldwide. Pick a continent, find countries on the map                                          |
| **Producers**           | The ten biggest producers, their share in the continent and the mix of coffee they grow                                            |
| **Trade & Consumption** | Production against consumption, a question of sustainability. Roast and ground versus instant coffee                               |
| **Country Profile**     | One country, one year or the whole period. Economic indicators, Year-end Stock and Production Change. Data sources are listed here |

---

### Good to know

- Commodity Coffee, Green, 1960 to 2023, 94 countries and regions
- Unit: 1000 bags of 60 kg, the Global Standard
- The European Union is one block in the data, so it is not on the map
- A PDF version is included for readers without Power BI.
- Croatia contains only zeros, a gap in the data
- For many countries consumption starts in 2002, so compare it with production from that year
- Europe is not a producer, only a processor

---

> Coffee starts on farms all over the world and ends in cups wherever it is brewed.
> The report shows who grows it, who drinks it and where those two worlds do not meet.
> It leads from the whole world down to one country and its numbers, from stocks to production change.
>
> For anyone who makes a coffee in the morning and wants to know where it came from, ideally before it goes cold.
